# The 7 Steps of the Design Thinking Process

Updated: April 4, 2025

![](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0259/7876/5396/files/design-thinking-process-steps-phases.jpg?v=1743457398)

[Design thinking](https://www.ideou.com/blogs/inspiration/what-is-design-thinking "what is design thinking") is a process for [creative problem solving](https://www.ideou.com/products/creative-problem-solving "creative problem solving course") that centers on the needs of people. It starts with what is desirable from a human point of view, along with what is technologically feasible and economically viable.

Whether you’re new to design thinking or revisiting it with fresh eyes, this guide offers both structure and inspiration.

## What is the Design Thinking Process?

With design thinking, the seven steps—framing a question, gathering inspiration, synthesizing for action, generating ideas, making ideas tangible, testing to learn, and sharing the story—form an iterative approach that is adapted for each specific challenge.

We teach the phases of design thinking as linear steps, but in practice the process is not always linear. Some of the phases may happen several times, and you may move back and forth between them. As you start learning about design thinking, it’s helpful to understand the seven phases of the process. Let’s dive into each step of the design thinking process.

## 7 Design Thinking Process Steps

Below, you’ll find all seven steps—each one explained with examples, activities, and takeaways to help you apply [design thinking](https://www.ideou.com/blogs/inspiration/what-is-design-thinking "design thinking guide") in real life.

### 1\. Frame a Question: Define the Challenge

![A picture frame set against a blue background.](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0259/7876/5396/files/DTProcess_Images_v4-01.png?v=1662065090)

In the design thinking process, it’s critical to determine the question that you’re asking before jumping to potential solutions. Start with curiosity and a beginner’s mindset. As you work to define a problem, “why?” questions can be helpful for uncovering a challenge or opportunity. [A great question](https://www.ideou.com/blogs/inspiration/how-the-right-question-can-lead-to-a-breakthrough) that led to the invention of the Polaroid Instant Camera in the 1940s was asked by the founder’s four-year-old daughter, who wondered, “Why do we have to wait for the picture?”

Taking the time to craft a good question will inspire your team and lead to stronger ideas that actually address the needs of your customers. Remember [the power of questions](https://www.ideou.com/blogs/inspiration/why-leadership-is-not-about-having-all-the-answers?_pos=3&_sid=f41050376&_ss=r)—they help you to learn more. Later on, “What if?” questions are effective for encouraging brainstorming, and “How?” questions are useful for narrowing in and implementing ideas. You’ll oftentimes return to this part of the process and ask questions at multiple points throughout your design thinking journey.

_Challenge yourself: [Lead through questions](https://www.ideou.com/blogs/inspiration/how-creative-leaders-tap-into-the-power-of-questions "Lead through questions")**[Use Thoughtful Questions to Drive Breakthrough Ideas](https://www.ideou.com/blogs/inspiration/how-creative-leaders-tap-into-the-power-of-questions)**_

### 2\. Gather Inspiration: **Research & Empathy in Action**

![A camera set against a red background.](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0259/7876/5396/files/DTProcess_Images_v4-02.png?v=1662065122)

An important piece of design thinking is drawing inspiration from what people really need. Observation, [interviews](https://www.ideou.com/blogs/inspiration/6-tips-from-ideo-designers-on-how-to-unlock-insightful-conversation?_pos=2&_sid=38ac8bebb&_ss=r), and surveys can help you to better understand the groups you’re working with. When you listen actively and step into another point of view, you [build empathy](https://www.ideou.com/blogs/inspiration/how-to-turn-empathy-into-your-secret-strength) in a way that allows you to bring in a diverse set of ideas and perspectives, which can lead to more creative solutions.

When you are gathering inspiration, focus on staying open to different contexts and ways of thinking. One activity that can be useful is [analogous inspiration,](https://www.ideou.com/blogs/inspiration/activity-explore-analogous-inspiration) which involves looking at analogous contexts outside of the particular field you’re working with to spark new insights. You can then start to brainstorm ideas with the insights you uncover through your research.

_Challenge yourself: [Explore analogous inspiration](https://www.ideou.com/blogs/inspiration/activity-explore-analogous-inspiration "Explore analogous inspiration")**[Discover Game-Changing Insights from Other Field](https://www.ideou.com/blogs/inspiration/activity-explore-analogous-inspiration)**_

### 3\. Synthesize for Action: Turn Research into Insights

![step 3 of the design thinking process](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0259/7876/5396/files/sythesize-for-action.png?v=1744312505)

After gathering research, it’s time to make sense of it all. This phase is about finding patterns, surfacing key themes, and uncovering insights that will guide your design solution. Think of it like assembling a puzzle—you’ve collected stories, emotions, and data, and now you’re looking for the connections.

A strong insight isn’t just an observation. It’s a shift in perspective—something that reveals an unmet need or sparks a new way of thinking. The best insights do three things:

- **Inform:** They reveal what people truly need or value

- **Inspire:** They motivate fresh ideas

- **Stick:** They’re memorable and easy to share


For example, instead of _“People find it hard to track their medication,”_ you might say:

_“People want medication management to feel effortless, not like another task on their to-do list.”_

This phrasing not only highlights the problem—it speaks to the emotional need behind it.

Once you’ve distilled your insights, turn them into " _How Might We_ **"** questions to guide your ideation. For example: _“How might we make managing medication feel effortless and integrated into daily life?”_

_Challenge yourself:  **Craft insight statements and turn them into "How Might We" questions**_

### 4\. Generate Ideas: Expand Creative Possibilities

![A light bulb set against a yellow background.](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0259/7876/5396/files/DTProcess_Images_v4-03.png?v=1662065145)

When [brainstorming](https://www.ideou.com/pages/brainstorming) new ideas, you diverge and explore all of the possible solutions to a problem before converging on the best ones. [Divergent thinking](https://www.ideou.com/blogs/inspiration/brendan-boyle-on-divergent-thinking-and-the-innovation-funnel) focuses on quantity over quality, prompting you to share ideas that might seem wild or unfeasible. Then, you narrow down with convergent thinking. You might diverge and converge many times before moving forward with an idea.

As you ideate with your team, keep in mind the [7 rules of brainstorming](https://www.ideou.com/blogs/inspiration/7-simple-rules-of-brainstorming), which include deferring judgment and building on the ideas of others. An activity that can encourage groups to be more creative and generative is the [magic circle](https://www.ideou.com/blogs/inspiration/magic-circle-activity-brainstorming-wild-ideas), where you momentarily set aside constraints and dream big. Afterward, you can use your constraints to evaluate and refine your ideas.

_Challenge yourself: [Brainstorm wild ideas](https://www.ideou.com/blogs/inspiration/magic-circle-activity-brainstorming-wild-ideas "Brainstorm wild ideas")**[Push Boundaries with Radical Brainstorming](https://www.ideou.com/blogs/inspiration/magic-circle-activity-brainstorming-wild-ideas)**_

### 5\. Make Ideas Tangible: Rapid Prototypes

![A pair of scissors set against a purple background.](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0259/7876/5396/files/DTProcess_Images_v4-04.png?v=1662065170)

[Prototypes](https://www.ideou.com/blogs/inspiration/why-everyone-should-prototype-not-just-designers "Prototypes") provide a way to communicate your ideas by making them tangible. They can take many forms, from a [sketch](https://www.ideou.com/blogs/page/make-your-ideas-tangible-through-prototypes "sketch") to a cardboard model. When you create a prototype, it allows you to get feedback from others and learn what’s working. In the process of building a rough version of your idea, you may even discover more ideas that you hadn’t thought of before.

There are many different [kinds of prototypes](https://www.ideou.com/blogs/inspiration/all-prototypes-are-not-created-equal "kinds of prototypes") that you can create. When getting started, be quick and scrappy using whatever tools you have. Once you build early prototypes, you can then test them with your users, and use your learnings to improve your prototypes.

_Challenge yourself: [Prototype with sketches](https://www.ideou.com/blogs/page/make-your-ideas-tangible-through-prototypes "Prototype with sketches")**[Sketch a Rapid Prototype in Minutes](https://www.ideou.com/blogs/page/make-your-ideas-tangible-through-prototypes)**_

### 6\. Test to Learn: Refine Solutions Through Feedback

![A clipboard set against a teal background.](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0259/7876/5396/files/DTProcess_Images_v4-05.png?v=1662065193)

When you test your prototypes, you learn about how people would interact with your ideas in the real world. As with prototyping, there are a [variety of tests](https://www.ideou.com/blogs/inspiration/build-confidence-in-your-strategy-with-small-tests "variety of tests") that you can run, from low-cost guerilla tests to higher-fidelity ones. Tests can give you quantitative metrics and evidence, as well as qualitative emotions and reactions from your users.

Oftentimes, what you learn in your tests will inform how you move forward with your ideas, and you’ll go through a few cycles of prototyping and testing before arriving at your solution. This is called [iteration](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/iteration "iteration definition"), and is a key part of the design thinking process. Refining your prototypes will bring you closer and closer to your finished product or service.

_Challenge yourself: [**R**](https://www.ideou.com/blogs/inspiration/build-confidence-in-your-strategy-with-small-tests "Run small tests") **[un Quick Tests to Refine Your Idea](https://www.ideou.com/blogs/inspiration/build-confidence-in-your-strategy-with-small-tests)**_

### 7\. Share the Story: Communicate Ideas That Resonate

![A microphone set against a green background.](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0259/7876/5396/files/DTProcess_Images_v4-06.png?v=1662065216)

Once you’ve taken your idea through prototyping and testing, craft your story and share it with colleagues, clients, and customers. [Establishing a culture of storytelling](https://www.ideou.com/blogs/inspiration/establishing-a-culture-of-storytelling "Storytelling") is an important part of the design thinking process because it connects you to the people you’re sharing your idea with, and makes it relatable on a human level. Without an engaging story, it’s hard for others to understand why your work matters.

When communicating your story, focus on the big idea that you’re trying to get across, and remember to put your audience at the center of it. [These 5 tips that make you better at storytelling](https://www.ideou.com/blogs/inspiration/5-tips-that-will-make-you-a-better-storyteller "These storytelling tips") will allow you to create a compelling story that resonates. As you continue to practice your design thinking skills, sharing your experience will help you to reflect and improve your craft. ## Key Takeaways

- Design Thinking is a flexible, iterative process that adapts to different challenges.
- Empathy is essential for understanding users' needs and creating meaningful solutions.
- Reframing the problem leads to more innovative and effective solutions.
- Prototyping helps bring ideas to life quickly and gather early feedback.
- Testing is a learning process that drives continuous improvement and iteration.
- Collaboration across diverse teams leads to stronger, more holistic solutions.

Storytelling enhances the impact and adoption of innovative ideas.
