**In 2026, wellness trends are all about becoming slow, simplified and specialized. From bespoke supplementation and community wellness to the move to the metaverse, here’s how to live well in 2026.**

We saw that [wellness got smart](https://www.forbes.com/sites/angelinavillaclarke/2025/02/04/wellness-trends-report-2025-how-wellness-got-smarter/) in 2025, with the rise of AI therapy, a focus on healthspan over lifespan and sleep optimisation. Now is the time to look ahead to what we’ll see trending in the wellness space in 2026.

New health trends including tech-free diets, human connection as a healer, and health in virtual reality will shake up the wellness space next year and beyond. Stay ahead of the trend this year and read on to find the top five wellness trends to watch in 2026, according to health experts.

## 1\. Specialized Supplementation

![Happy smiling woman holding an omega pill in her hand.](https://imageio.forbes.com/specials-images/imageserve/694aaadcc18d0c856217848e/Happy-smiling-woman-holding-an-omega-pill-in-her-hand-/960x0.jpg?format=jpg&width=144)

This year is all about taking a bespoke approach to your vitamins and nutrition.

getty

Say goodbye to the one-vitamin-fits-all approach to your supplements. This year is all about taking a bespoke approach to your vitamins and nutrition. Thanks to the rise of functional medicine, women are going back to basics and relying on food for nutrients, alongside medical testing to find deficiencies in their diet and building a customized supplements plan, tailor made for their own health.

“Physicians trained in the functional medicine method are not just treating symptoms – the old ‘one drug fits all’ approach – but are looking for root causes of those symptoms,” said longevity expert Leslie Kenny. “The future of supplementation is bespoke – not in luxury, but in logic. As we understand more about epigenome, microbiome, and lifestyle stressors, it makes sense our supplement routines should reflect that individuality, based on what blood and urine panels reveal about our vitamin, mineral, antioxidant and hormone deficiencies or imbalances,” she said.

To get testing, visit a functional medicine doctor trained in conducting a full history intake where all your symptoms, large and small, are cataloged, Leslie advised. From there, your practitioner should recommend certain tests to get to the root cause issues.

## 2\. This Is The Tech Abstinence Movement

With the always-on screen culture of social media doomscrolling and content consumption causing [digital burnout and stress](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12192724/) according to research, we’ll see a counter movement towards more rest and slow tech. “Women (94%) were more likely than men (89%) to experience high or extreme levels of pressure or stress in the past year,” [according to Mental Health U.K](https://mentalhealth-uk.org/burnout/).

From device-free rituals, sleep hacks, the return of analog alarm clocks and banning phones in bed, to screen time monitors, digital detox retreats and popular trends like [slowmaxxing](https://living360.uk/slowmaxxing-tiktok-trend-burnout-mental-health/) – it’s finally time to turn off the tech.

“As humans, a slower and more connected pace is what we crave fundamentally, as our default is presence,” said Sarie Taylor, psychotherapist and founder of Worldwide Wellbeing. “The benefits of this trend are not only limitless, but they take us back to simplicity, making it much easier to sustain much more helpful habits in a short space of time.”

Practicing tech abstinence has a knock-on effect in many other areas of your health and can lead to better sleep, more clarity and brain capacity for other tasks. “Prevention is definitely better than cure when it comes to stress and burnout,” said Taylor.

## 3\. The Rise Of Community Wellness And Connection As A Healer

![Group, back and friends to relax outdoor on school break, lens flare and hug for bonding in summer. People, students and embrace with support, care and trust together for social reunion at park](https://imageio.forbes.com/specials-images/imageserve/694aab449dd451c60dea8e4e/Group--back-and-friends-to-relax-outdoor-on-school-break--lens-flare-and-hug-for/960x0.jpg?format=jpg&width=144)

A shared approach to wellness, powered by community and connection, will become the new way to get well.

getty

Due to so much focus on an individual approach to wellness – ‘self-care’ has become a buzzword in the industry, the subject of more than [19 million TikTok posts](https://www.tiktok.com/tag/SelfCare) – it’s not surprising that optimizing your health alone can tend to feel, well, lonely. By contrast, [research](https://hsph.harvard.edu/news/the-importance-of-connections-ways-to-live-a-longer-healthier-life/) links social connection to better health and longevity.

That’s why a shared approach to wellness, powered by community and connection, will become the new way to get well.

“After years of remote work, self-improvement in isolation, and hyper-digital living, people are realizing that true wellness depends on connection,” said movement specialist and author Sarah Aspinall. “Women in particular are craving spaces where they can belong, be seen, and move together – a counterbalance to the individualism and optimization culture of the past decade.”

In 2026 and beyond, we’ll see more multi-generational community wellness spaces, particularly for women across important life stages such as new motherhood and menopause, as well as seeking out third spaces for local get-togethers and group activities around a shared interest that spark connection.

“This is the era of ‘social health’ – where community becomes medicine. When women move together, their brains release oxytocin and endorphins, chemicals that lower stress and strengthen trust,” Aspinall said.

## 4\. Welcome To Wellness In The Metaverse

Although we’ll see a pushback on tech in our wellness routines, there is still a healthy appetite for blending health, fitness and mental wellbeing with digital technology. From immersive virtual reality workouts and meditation, to virtual spa retreats, blockchain platforms rewarding you with tokens for physical activity and even therapy delivered by AI avatars — this is wellness in the metaverse.

“The VR healthcare market is growing significantly — forecasted to grow approximately 30% per year through 2030 — as clinical professionals and researchers increasingly validate virtual environments as effective tools for reducing stress, enhancing physical therapy and fitness activities,” said Dr. Blen Tesfu, MD and medical advisor.

Research suggests that virtual environments can increase health outcomes, including VR mindfulness programs which have reduced participants’ stress and anxiety levels and virtual exercise which resulted in more motivation and consistency than traditional exercise classes, Dr. Tesfu explained.

“For women, this trend provides an opportunity for convenient, inclusive, and supportive access to wellness experiences. These virtual wellness spaces allow women to participate in guided fitness classes, mindfulness sessions or health workshops regardless of their location or scheduling conflicts, which is particularly important for women who work or are caregivers,” she said.

## 5\. Skincare Simplified

![Smiling mixed race young woman applying moisturizer on her face in bathroom](https://imageio.forbes.com/specials-images/imageserve/694aaa8dd7f31d4a09e1700a/Smiling-mixed-race-young-woman-applying-moisturizer-on-her-face-in-bathroom/960x0.jpg?format=jpg&width=144)

In 2026 we’ll see a rise in the minimalist approach to skincare.

getty

If you’re feeling exhausted by your 12-step skincare routine, you’re not alone. From impossible skincare standards like the ‘glass skin look’ to complicated, multi-step rituals, women are feeling fatigued and crave a simpler, more basic approach to healthy skin. And the [research](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11381309/) shows that too many personal care products, even those with “natural” ingredients, can be detrimental and even pose hazards to your health.

So, we’ll see a rise in the minimalist approach to skincare, with a focus on quality products made with only quality, sustainably sourced ingredients that boost your skin’s unique needs with ease and little effort.

“The science favors barrier friendly basics and fewer irritants,” said dermatologist Dr. Shamsa Kanwal, MD. “Harsh surfactants and over-cleansing disrupt your skin’s proteins and lipids, raising irritation and transepidermal water loss, which supports a move to gentler, simpler routines.”

To nourish your skin without the frills, Dr. Kanwal suggested simplifying your skincare to just 3-4 steps: “The recommended routine is: cleanse gently, moisturize, use SPF 30+ daily, and layer in only one targeted active at a time.”

She also recommended choosing fragrance-free formulas to reduce the risk of contact allergens, and add active ingredients like niacinamide for barrier support or ceramides for lipid replenishment as you need it.
